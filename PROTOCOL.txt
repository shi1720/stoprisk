StopRisk computational protocol, frozen before the main experimental run
17 October 2026. This is an internal prospective record, not a registered study.

Question: How do finite counts constrain harm-before-catch probability when an
attacker chooses adaptively among a fixed, tested menu with stationary kernels?

Primary controlled experiment: fixed n per action in {50,200,1000,5000},
10000 independently simulated evaluation datasets per condition, seed 1720406.
Horizon 100. Six prespecified two-action menus (u,c):
balanced [(0.02,0.08),(0.01,0.02)]
rare [(0.0002,0.001),(0.0001,0.0001)]
zero-harm [(0,0.01),(0,0.1)]
switching [(0.05,0.5),(0.01,0.01)]
low-catch [(0.01,0.0001),(0.005,0.0002)]
asymmetric [(0.001,0.3),(0.03,0.05)].
Compare separately calibrated 95% simultaneous factor rectangles and hazard
rectangles. Primary endpoints: coverage of true optimal finite-horizon risk,
mean interval width, mean signed plug-in error, frequency of zero plug-in
estimate when true risk is positive. Report Monte Carlo standard errors.
No claim of uniform empirical superiority is a success criterion.

Exact verification: enumerate all two-action count tables for n=8 per action
and evaluate coverage at horizons 1,5,20 for the same six menus. Include
single-policy exact conditional percentile-bootstrap upper-bound coverage
for p=(u,c,continue), n=100, horizon 100, across the same action kernels.
This bootstrap baseline is conditional on the decisive sample size and
re-estimates r, while holding the empirical decisive frequency fixed.
It is explicitly NOT a replication of ControlArena's raw-row bootstrap.
